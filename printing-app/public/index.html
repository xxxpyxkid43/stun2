Day 1 – Theory
Learning Sublimation & Vinyl Print types — Theory.
Lesson 1What is Sublimation Printing?▾
  • 🔬

    The Science

    Solid dye turns to gas under heat/pressure (180–210°C, 40–60 PSI), bonding permanently with polyester fibres or polymer-coated surfaces. Result: vivid, wash-resistant prints.

  • 🧵

    Compatible Substrates

    Works on 100% polyester fabric, polymer-coated mugs, coated aluminium, ceramic tiles, mouse pads. Does NOT work on 100% cotton.

  • 🖨️

    The Sublimation Printer

    Uses special sublimation ink. Print a MIRRORED image onto transfer paper, press face-down on your substrate in the heat press.

💡 Key Point

Always mirror your design — otherwise the result will appear flipped on your product.

Lesson 2What is Vinyl Printing?▾
  • ✂️

    How Vinyl Cutting Works

    A vinyl cutting plotter uses a blade to cut shapes/text from vinyl sheets. Weed away excess vinyl, apply transfer tape, then stick to the surface.

  • 🔥

    Heat Transfer Vinyl (HTV)

    Applied to fabric using a heat press at 150–165°C for 10–15 seconds. The heat activates the adhesive backing permanently.

  • 🪟

    Iron-On Vinyl

    Home-friendly alternative. Use a domestic iron at high heat (no steam) pressing firmly for 30–60 seconds. Best for small designs.

💡 Key Point

Mirror HTV designs before cutting — you place vinyl shiny-side down on fabric.

Day 2 – Practical
Hands-on with machines — sublimation on T-shirts & cups, vinyl on glass, wooden boards.
Practical 1Sublimation on T-Shirts▾
  • 1️⃣

    Prepare & Mirror Design

    Create full-colour design, resize to A4 chest size, mirror horizontally before printing to sublimation paper.

  • 2️⃣

    Pre-Press the Shirt

    Press 5 seconds at 200°C to remove moisture and wrinkles. Creates a flat even surface.

  • 3️⃣

    Apply & Press

    Position paper face-down. Secure with heat tape. Close press at 200°C / 50 PSI for 45–60 seconds.

  • 4️⃣

    Reveal

    Peel paper while warm (hot peel). Lay flat to cool. Vivid colours now embedded in fabric.

💡 Pro Tip

Use a silicone mat under the shirt to prevent seam lines appearing in your print.

Practical 2Sublimation on Cups & Mugs▾
  • ☕

    Use Polymer-Coated Mugs

    Only sublimation blanks work. White base gives truest colours. Standard 11oz wrap = approx 20×9 cm.

  • 🔧

    Mug Press Settings

    Wrap transfer around mug, secure with heat tape. Press at 190°C for 3–4 minutes rotating evenly.

  • 💧

    Cool & Finish

    Peel paper immediately. Rinse under cold water. Inspect quality. Allow to dry fully before use.

Practical 3Vinyl on Glass & Iron-On Application▾
  • 🪟

    Adhesive Vinyl on Glass

    Clean with isopropyl alcohol. Weed design, apply transfer tape, slowly smooth from one edge to avoid bubbles. Squeegee firmly.

  • 🧺

    Iron-On HTV on Fabric

    Cut HTV shiny-side up, weed, flip shiny-side down. Cover with parchment paper. Iron at high heat, no steam, 45 seconds firmly.

Practical 4Printing on Wooden Boards▾
  • 🪵

    Prepare Coated Board

    Must be polymer-coated. Sand lightly, wipe clean. Bold high-contrast designs work best on wood grain.

  • ♨️

    Press Settings

    Press at 190–200°C for 50–70 seconds. Firm even pressure. Peel immediately. Apply food-safe sealant if used for food contact.

💡 Business Tip

Personalised wooden boards sell for R200–R500 and take under 10 minutes to produce — great margin!

Common Questions

Q: What machines do I need to start?
A: Sublimation printer (converted Epson), heat press, vinyl cutter. Budget R15,000–R25,000.

Q: Can I use a regular inkjet printer?
A: No — you need a printer specifically for sublimation ink.

Q: How much can I charge per product?
A: Custom mug R80–R150. T-shirt R150–R300. Wooden board R200–R500.

Glossary
Key terms throughout the Printing Masterclass.
Sublimation
Solid dye converts to gas under heat, bonding permanently with polyester or polymer-coated surfaces.
Heat Press
Machine applying heat and pressure to transfer images from paper to a substrate.
Vinyl Cutter / Plotter
Machine using a small blade to cut shapes from vinyl sheets, guided by design software.
HTV (Heat Transfer Vinyl)
Vinyl with heat-activated adhesive backing for permanent bonding to fabric.
Weeding
Removing unwanted negative vinyl from around your cut design.
Transfer Paper
Sublimation: coated paper holding ink before pressing. Vinyl: clear adhesive sheet to relocate cut designs.
Substrate
The surface being printed on — T-shirt, mug, glass, wooden board, etc.
Polymer Coating
Thin synthetic coating on non-polyester surfaces allowing sublimation ink to bond.
Mirror / Flip
Horizontally reversing design before printing so the final transfer appears correctly oriented.
PSI
Pounds per square inch — unit of pressure used in heat press settings (typically 40–60 PSI).
Teflon Sheet
Non-stick protective sheet between the heat press platen and garment for even heat distribution.
Outgassing
Gases released from a heated substrate that can create bubbles — prevented by pre-pressing.
